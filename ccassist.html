Character Creation Assistance
A guide dedicated to new players building their first character!



Meet Your DM Team
The task of creating your very first character can be a daunting challenge, especially if this is your very first time ever playing in a TTRPG. Thankfully, the DM team has gone through the process of making a character hundreds of times, and has created this curated guide to assist you in creating a character. As far as rulesets go, we use the 2024 fifth edition rules. We allow all official Wizards of the Coast character options, as well as all Critical Role third-party content to be used for character creation.

Step 1. Decide on the theme of your character. Who are they? What drives them to adventure? What do they look like? And most importantly, what Species, Background, and Class fit them the best to have the most immersive experiance possible. If someone were to look at you, what would they immedietly be able to tell is something you would be skilled at?

Step 2. Decide on your class. This is the thing that will define how your character interacts with the world mechanically in matters of combat and utility, so it is the most important choice you will be making when making your character. However, don't feel bogged down by the fact a class might be able to do more damage than another. Every class can fight well and contribute meaningfully to a team. If you are looking for a class that is simple to understand and play, while still feeling rewarding. A list of all classes available and their subclasses can be found on 5e Tools. If you are looking for a simple but rewarding class, we recommend Barbarian. It operates well in combat and in it's utility, and is always good to have around.

Step 3. Decide on your species. While the race you choose will give you different abilities, any race can be any class and still work very well. The most important thing that this will really drive, is your origins. The Woven Lands and her people has a long and storied history, and this decision will assist you in defining the origin for your character and their culture. A List of all species available can be found on 5e Tools.

Step 4. Now that you have your class and race selected, let's go over stats! We allow the use of both Standard Array as well as the Point Buy method, but it is up to player preference. Standard array uses the numbers 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, and 8. Point Buy allows you to set each score at 8, and essentially raise them by spending skill points. A handy calculator for building a character via point buy can be found here.

Step 5. Now for the finishing touch of your character, selecting their background. This, moreso than even your species, can really show what type of person your character is. This will also give you an ability score increase that you can use to even further increase the scores that we just put on our character sheet! A list of available character backgrounds can be found on 5e Tools.
